Prints snap too easily / brittle

Strength & Structure Material + slicer
⚡ Check this first: Dry the filament and raise temp 10°C. Brittle PLA is usually wet or old; weak layer bonds are usually too-cold printing.

What it looks like

Parts snap with little force, often cleanly along layer lines. PLA that used to be fine now breaks when you flex it. Or a spool of filament snaps when you bend it by hand.

Causes, most likely first

  1. Wet filament — moisture destroys PLA's toughness; wet PLA snaps like dry spaghetti.
  2. Old/degraded PLA — years-old spools become brittle even stored well.
  3. Printing too cold — weak inter-layer bonds (see Layer separation).
  4. Too few walls / low infill for a functional part.
  5. Wrong material for the job — PLA is inherently brittle; functional parts that take impacts want PETG or ASA.

Fixes, in the order to try them

  1. Dry the spool — if hand-bending the raw filament snaps it, dry it; if it still snaps after drying, the spool is done.
  2. Raise temp 5–10°C for stronger layer bonds.
  3. More walls (4–6) rather than more infill for strength.
  4. Switch materials for functional parts: PETG for toughness, ASA for heat + toughness.
  5. Store PLA sealed with desiccant — brittleness is usually a storage story.

Related issues

  • Dimensional inaccuracy — parts don't fit
  • Gaps between infill and walls
  • Layer separation / splitting (delamination)
  • Layer shifting — layers misaligned
  • Poor bridging — sagging strands across gaps
  • Rough / drooping overhangs
  • Poor surface above supports
  • Weak, stringy infill
